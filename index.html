Magic Metrics: The Key to Harry Potter's Success

This project investigated what really drives commercial success in the Harry Potter film franchise — testing whether narrative factors (like cast size) or production factors (like budget) better explain box office performance. I hypothesized upfront that the number of unique characters in a film would have no meaningful relationship to its commercial or critical success, then tested that assumption against real data rather than assuming it.

Comparative Benchmarking Study of YOLOv5 and YOLOv8

An empirical computer vision study evaluating the training stability, computational requirements, and real-time inference efficiency of anchor-based (YOLOv5) versus anchor-free (YOLOv8) deep learning architectures. Using high-resolution aerial drone imagery, the project benchmarked three scaling sizes (Nano, Small, Medium) across 18 controlled training sessions to map model convergence and out-of-sample generalization.

Neural Networks and Convolutional Neural Network

A comparative deep learning study evaluating the performance and spatial feature extraction capabilities of a multi-layer Feedforward Neural Network (FNN) against a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). Implemented using PyTorch, both architectures were trained to classify handwritten digits from the USPS dataset and evaluated using a stratified 5-fold cross-validation strategy to assess out-of-sample generalization.

From-Scratch Neural Network for PII Detection

Our team designed and presented a multi-layer neural network built entirely from scratch in NumPy to classify whether business documents contain Personally Identifiable Information (PII) — including names, SSNs, credit card numbers, and other regulated data types. The project was framed around a real business problem: organizations face severe legal and financial exposure from PII mishandling, underscored by breaches like Change Healthcare (2024, ~192.7M records) and National Public Data (2024, 2.9B records)
